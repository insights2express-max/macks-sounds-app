Sound Prep v334

Turn a long recording into a short loop that repeats without a gap or click. Your original file is never changed: the loop is saved as a new file.

← Mack's Sounds Open in its own tab ↗

1 Open a sound

How this step works

WAV, MP3 or any sound file this browser can play. Large WAV files (even 30-minute ones) open without loading the whole file into memory.

or drop a file anywhere on this page

2 Choose the stretch to loop

How this step works

Drag across the picture of the sound to choose a stretch, or type where it starts and how long it is. Drag a green or red line to move one end. The mouse wheel zooms; Shift + wheel scrolls. Pick a stretch that sounds typical, without a one-off noise in it.

Quick length:
Moves both ends inward past any silence. Useful for a one-off sound such as a thunder roll; a steady sound has none.

3 Find the best join

How this step works

Looks near both ends for the spot where the end of the loop flows best into its start: the same sound pattern on both sides, at a quiet moment, starting and ending where the wave crosses zero. For a steady rhythm such as a clock, the length becomes a whole number of beats.

Fine-tune: Start End

4 Repair clipped peaks and clicks optional

How this step works

A recording that was too loud when it was made can crack at its loudest moments: the tops of its biggest waves were cut flat (clipping, still flat even if the file was turned down afterward), or the wave snaps sharply for a moment (crackle). This finds those spots in the loop and redraws each one from the sound around it. Only those few samples change; your original file never does. Natural sharp sounds, such as a clock's tick or a frog's croak, are left alone, and on a clean recording it finds nothing. Leave it off unless you hear a crack.

Selected spot: Plays 2 s around the spot. Click a row to select it and show it up close in the picture in step 2 (the repaired wave is drawn in orange).

Repaired spots

5 Blend the join

How this step works

Fades the last moments of the loop into the sound that comes just before its start, so the join is smooth even when the two ends don't match exactly. The loop's length doesn't change.

6 Check the join

How this step works

Measured on the finished loop, at the exact spot where its end meets its start. A click is a sudden jump, so the jump at the join is compared with how much the sound normally moves from one sample to the next. Up to 3× is normal movement (no click); a level difference under 3 dB isn't noticeable.

Close-up of the join

7 Even loudness

How this step works

Sets the loop to the same loudness as the player's Auto level (), measured the same way, so it sits evenly with your other sounds. It never turns a sound up past the point where its loudest moment would distort.

8 Listen to it repeat

How this step works

Plays the loop over and over, each time starting on the exact sample where the last one ended, the way the player does. "Hear the join" starts 3 seconds before the end so you reach the join quickly.

While it plays, the line in step 2's picture shows where you are.

Playing

9 Save the loop

How this step works

Saves the finished loop as a new WAV file. Then add it to a mix in Mack's Sounds with ➕ Add Local and switch its Loop on.
